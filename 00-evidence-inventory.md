# TPC Performance OS: Knowledge-Base Inventory & Evidence Extraction

## 1. Clinical Operating Philosophy & System Foundation

The foundational principle of the TPC Performance OS second-clinician system is that **imperfect or incomplete evidence does not warrant withholding reasonable, low-risk, and plausible interventions.** Clinical decisions must balance physiological plausibility, athlete goals, reversibility, and individual response monitoring against potential risks. 

To maintain strict source traceability, the evidence base is structured without inventing unsupported threshold numbers, clinical cutoffs, or arbitrary programming rules.

## 2. Structured Research Coverage Matrix

### 2.1 Running Physiology & Energetics
* **Topics Covered:** Maximal oxygen uptake (\\(\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}\\)), velocity at \\(\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}\\) (\\(v\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}\\)), Maximal Aerobic Speed (MAS), Lactate Threshold (LT), \\(D_{\max}\\) lactate curve modeling, Ventilatory Thresholds (VT1/VT2), Critical Speed (CS), Anaerobic Running Capacity / Speed Reserve, and Running Economy (RE).
* **Supporting Sources:** *Developing Endurance*, *Daniels' Running Formula*, *Llanos-Lagos et al. (202 Meta-Analysis)*, *Llanos-Lagos et al. (2024 Meta-Analysis)*.
* **Applicable Population:** Middle-distance (800m–3000m), long-distance (5km–marathon), and ultra-endurance runners across recreational, well-trained, and elite cohorts.
* **Limitations & Context:** Laboratory metabolic measures require specialized equipment (metabolic cart, blood lactate meters). Field surrogates (e.g., 5-minute time trial for MAS, 4-time-trial curve for CS) provide practical estimates but introduce protocol-dependent variance.
* **Support Level:** **Direct Support** for energy system dynamics and threshold definitions; **Clinical Interpretation** required when assigning precise training zones from submaximal field tests.

### 2.2 Running Biomechanics & Movement Analysis
* **Topics Covered:** 2D video-based planar kinematics (initial contact, stance phase loading, flight phase), spatial-temporal variables (cadence, step length, ground contact time), vertical displacement of center of mass, peak knee flexion (~45° at stance), pelvic obliquity/drop, dynamic knee valgus, dynamic foot whip angle, and strike patterns.
* **Supporting Sources:** *EBP for Biomechanical Analysis (Souza)*, *Developing Endurance*.
* **Applicable Population:** Distance runners undergoing movement screening or gait retraining for performance or injury management.
* **Limitations & Context:** Validated biomechanical cutoffs predictive of injury **do not exist** in the literature; variables must be visually or frame-by-frame identified and judged against individual normative ranges. Treadmill gait requires 6–10 minutes of acclimation before kinematic stability is reached.
* **Support Level:** **Direct Support** for measurement protocols and qualitative indicators; **Requires Clinical Interpretation** for identifying "abnormalities" and linking them to tissue stress or injury mechanics.

### 2.3 Physical Capacity & Gym-Based Strength & Conditioning
* **Topics Covered:** Lower-body maximal strength, rate of force development (RFD), reactive strength index (RSI), tendon/leg stiffness, impulse, explosive power, single-leg strength/stability, and trunk/core structural robustness.
* **Supporting Sources:** *Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning*, *High-Performance Training for Sports*, *Developing Endurance*, *NSCA's Guide to Program Design*.
* **Applicable Population:** Middle- and long-distance runners across training ages and competitive tiers.
* **Limitations & Context:** High-speed force production tests (e.g., IMTP, force plate CMJ) require specific laboratory hardware. Heavy strength training does not induce unwanted muscle hypertrophy in endurance-trained athletes when combined with high running volumes.
* **Support Level:** **Direct Support** for neuromuscular adaptation mechanisms and test administration; **Clinical Interpretation** required to balance concurrent training fatigue.

### 2.4 Injury Assessment, Load Management & Systemic Health
* **Topics Covered:**
  * **Systemic Energy Availability:** Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs), Low Energy Availability (LEA) calculations, Low Carbohydrate Availability (LCA) magnifying effects, REDs Clinical Assessment Tool (IOC REDs CAT2) 4-tier traffic light system (Green, Yellow, Orange, Red), and Male Athlete REDs indicators (decreased morning erections, low libido).
  * **Load Management:** Internal vs. External workload monitoring, Acute-to-Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR) and Banister model principles, <10% weekly workload progression rule-of-thumb, and horizontal alternation.
  * **Health & Injury Surveillance:** Standardized consensus definition of running injury (musculoskeletal pain causing modified training for \\(\ge 7\\) days or 3 consecutive sessions), bone stress injury (BSI) risk domains, and health history screening.
* **Supporting Sources:** *IOC REDs Consensus Statement (Mountjoy et al. 2023)*, *IOC Load & Illness Consensus (Schwellnus et al. 2016)*, *AMSSM Runner Health Surveillance (Tenforde et al. 2023)*, *High-Performance Training for Sports*, *Essentials of STC*.
* **Applicable Population:** Male and female endurance runners, adolescent through senior/master categories, including Para athletes.
* **Limitations & Context:** Mathematical coupling in ACWR calculations can introduce spurious correlations. Systemic REDs diagnosis requires ruling out non-LEA differential diagnoses via a multidisciplinary medical team.
* **Support Level:** **Direct Support** for standardized injury definitions, REDs traffic-light criteria, and health surveillance items; **Requires Clinical Interpretation** for risk-benefit participation decisions in Yellow/Orange zones.

### 2.5 Exercise Prescription & Periodization Models
* **Topics Covered:** Training Intensity Distributions (Polarized vs. Pyramidal TIDs across Moderate, Heavy, Severe, and Extreme domains), Block Periodization (Accumulation, Transmutation, Realization), Conjugate Sequencing, Summated Microcycle (3:1 loading/unloading blocks), Tapering, and Concurrent Strength & Endurance Periodization.
* **Supporting Sources:** *Developing Endurance*, *NSCA's Guide to Program Design*, *High-Performance Training for Sports*, *Daniels' Running Formula*.
* **Applicable Population:** Distance runners from novice/recreational (<30 miles/wk) to well-trained/elite (>50–70+ miles/wk).
* **Limitations & Context:** Elite pyramidal distribution (80/15/5) may under-stimulate recreational runners who have lower total volume; sub-elite runners require a higher proportion of hard/severe domain work relative to total volume.
* **Support Level:** **Direct Support** for periodization models and microcycle loading structures; **Requires Clinical Interpretation** for individual volume/intensity scaling.

## 4. Gym-Based S&C Physical Qualities & Supported Interventions

### 4.1 Modifiable Physical Qualities
1. **Maximal Muscular Strength:** High-load resistance training increases motor unit recruitment, firing rate, and intra/intermuscular coordination, reducing the relative effort required at submaximal running speeds.
2. **Reactive Strength & Muscle-Tendon Stiffness:** Plyometric and jump training enhance tendon elastic strain energy storage and recoil (specifically ankle plantarflexors and Achilles tendon complex), shortening ground contact time.
3. **Rate of Force Development (RFD) & Explosive Power:** High-load and fast-velocity movements expand the force-velocity ceiling, accelerating the transition from braking to propulsive phases during running gait.
4. **Structural Robustness & Tissue Capacity:** Unilateral strength (e.g., split squats, step-ups) and targeted core/calf-solues loading improve kinetic chain force absorption and build capacity in vulnerable injury zones.

### 4.2 Supported Loading Strategies, Interventions & Periodization
* **High-Load Resistance Training (\\(\ge 80\%\\) 1RM / \\(\le 6\\) RM):** 
  * *Evidence:* Statistically significant **moderate positive effect** on running performance (time trial and time to exhaustion) (\\(ES = -0.469, p = 0.029\\)) and **small-to-moderate positive effect** on Running Economy (\\(ES = -0.266, p = 0.039\\)).
  * *Moderator Insights:* High-load strength training is **most effective at higher running speeds (\\(>12\text{ km/h}\\))** and in runners possessing a **well-developed \\(\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}\\)**.
  * *Prescription:* 2–3 sets of 3–6 reps at \\(80–90\%\\) 1RM with 2–3 minutes rest, 2 days per week.
* **Plyometric Training (Light/Bodyweight Jump Training):**
  * *Evidence:* Statistically significant improvement in Running Economy **specifically at lower speeds (\\(\le 12\text{ km/h}\\))** (\\(ES = -0.307, p = 0.028\\)). However, plyometrics *alone* showed a smaller, non-significant effect on time trial performance (\\(ES = -0.210, p = 0.064\\)).
  * *Prescription:* Low-to-moderate volume hopping, bounding, depth jumps, and hurdle jumps (e.g., 3–4 sets of 5–10 ground contacts) integrated into warm-ups or dedicated units.
* **Combined / Complex Methods (High Load + Plyometrics):**
  * *Evidence:* Produces the **largest beneficial effect** on running performance (\\(ES = -1.035, p = 0.036\\)) and Running Economy (\\(ES = -0.426, p = 0.018\\)), outperforming single-method isolated training.
  * *Prescription:* Complex pairing of heavy bilateral/unilateral compound lifts immediately followed by explosive plyometric jumps or bounds.
* **Submaximal Load Training (40–79% 1RM) & Isometric Training:**
  * *Evidence:* Neither submaximal load training nor isolated isometric strength training elicited significant improvements in Running Economy or distance running performance compared to control groups (\\(p > 0.131\\)).
* **Metabolic Neutrality of S&C:** 
  * Strength training methods (High Load, Plyometrics, or Combined) **do not alter** \\(\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}\\), \\(v\dot{V}\text{O}_2\text{max}\\), Maximum Metabolic Steady State (lactate threshold), or sprint capacity (\\(all\ p > 0.072\\)). Performance gains occur via neuromuscular efficiency, mechanical stiffness, and non-metabolic adaptations.

## 5. Knowledge Gaps, Methodological Uncertainties & Areas of Disagreement

### 5.1 Areas of Disagreement & Literature Weaknesses
1. **Plyometric Meta-Analytic Discrepancies:** Conflict exists between prior meta-analyses claiming plyometrics alone significantly improves time-trial performance and the comprehensive 2024 meta-analysis by Llanos-Lagos et al. showing non-significant effects (\\(p = 0.064\\)), likely due to broader study inclusion criteria and meta-analytical modeling differences.
2. **Speed-Dependent Running Economy Adaptation:** High-load training selectively enhances RE at higher speeds (\\(>12\text{ km/h}\\)), whereas plyometrics selectively enhances RE at lower speeds (\\(\le 12\text{ km/h}\\)). Prescribing strength training without matching the athlete's primary racing or training pace is a common clinical flaw.
3. **Inconsistency of Submaximal Muscular Endurance Training:** Traditional gym prescriptions for distance runners (light loads, 15–25+ reps) are **unsupported** by meta-analytic evidence for improving RE or time-trial performance.
4. **Biomechanical Cutoff Deficits:** No prospectively validated angle or force cutoffs exist to classify a kinematic movement pattern as definitively "pathological" or predictive of injury.
5. **ACWR Methodological Validity:** Conventional ACWR models face criticism for mathematical coupling, potential false positives, and improper application across non-team sport populations.

### 5.2 Required Additional Evidence Categories
To transition from the current evidence library into a fully defensible, automated TPC Performance OS clinical engine, the following data categories must be incorporated:
1. **Advanced Shoe Technology (Super Shoes):** Longitudinal biomechanical and metabolic interaction data between thick-stack, carbon-plated footwear and lower-limb mechanical stiffness/Achilles tendon stress.
2. **Tendon Morphological & Structural Imaging:** Ultrasound/EPI normative databases for Achilles and patellar tendon cross-sectional area, stiffness, and strain thresholds.
3. **Force-Plate Normative Databases for Runners:** Specific IMTP and CMJ force-time curve reference values stratified by distance running competitive tiers (Tiers 2–5) rather than team-sport athletes.
4. **Sex-Specific LEA Thresholds:** Empirical dose-response curves establishing the exact energy availability cutoffs (\\(\text{kcal/kg FFM/day}\\)) for male runners experiencing hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis suppression.

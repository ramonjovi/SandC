# TPC test day guide (no force plate)

The same instructions are in the app under **Test protocols**. Tick marks show how well each procedure is backed:
**[S]** read in a supplied source, **[P]** the clinic's proposed standard (not in the books). Whatever you choose for a [P] test, keep it **identical at every retest**: these tests are tracked against the athlete's own baseline, never against a norm.

## Before you start
- Collect the screening questions and training history first. Do not test an athlete who is under medical review for a screening flag; do not test a painful area.
- Test at the **same time of day, indoors, on the same floor and treadmill** each time. [S, NSCA Essentials ch. 18]
- Give a short **familiarisation** 1-3 days before if the athlete has never done the tests, explain each test and **demonstrate it**. [S]
- Tell the athlete the result after each trial. [S]

## Equipment checklist
Treadmill and safety clip · barbell, plates, squat rack, safety bars or spotter · phone (120-240 fps) and tripod · tape measure · floor tape · small step or flat floor and a wall or rail · stopwatch or metronome · padded surface and an ankle strap or partner · optional stickers or tape for skin markers.

## Suggested running order (one session, about 60-75 minutes)
Follows the NSCA principle that no test should hurt the next one: non-fatiguing and skilled tests first, maximal strength next, fatiguing tests last, and exhausting aerobic tests on a different day. [S for the principle; the exact order below is a clinic suggestion]

1. Body mass (needed for every ratio)
2. Treadmill running video (easy, 6-10 min settle + filming)
3. Countermovement jump (3 jumps)
4. Standing broad jump (3 jumps)
5. Pogo hops for reactive strength
6. Balance reach (SEBT / Y-balance)
7. Back squat 3-5RM
8. Single-leg calf raise to fatigue
9. Nordic hamstring lowering (last: it causes soreness)
10. **Separate day:** race or time trial for VDOT

## 1. Back squat 3-5RM [S]
**Why:** sets every starting load and shows a strength deficit.
**Equipment:** barbell, plates, rack, safety bars or spotters, stopwatch.
1. Pick one squat depth (for example thighs parallel) and use it every time.
2. Warm up with a load that gives an easy 5-10 reps. Rest 1 minute.
3. Add 10-20%, do 3-5 reps. Rest 2 minutes.
4. Add 10-20%, do a near-maximal 2-3 reps. Rest 2-4 minutes.
5. Add 10-20% and attempt the target reps (3 or 5). If you get them, rest 2-4 minutes and add 5-10%; if not, drop the load slightly and retry.
6. Find the 3RM/5RM within **3-5 test sets** so fatigue does not distort the result.

**Enter:** squat test load (kg) and reps (keep reps at 5 or fewer: estimates from higher reps overestimate). **Stop** on pain or loss of technique. Use a 3-5RM, not a 1RM, for novices.

## 2. Treadmill running video [S, Souza 2016]
**Why:** observe running mechanics and any link to symptoms. Observations only: no cutoffs predict injury.
**Equipment:** treadmill, phone (120+ fps), tripod, optional skin markers (greater trochanter, knee, ankle, heel; PSIS for pelvic drop).
1. Set the speed to **the speed that brings on symptoms**; for a healthy runner use **long-run pace** (not race pace).
2. Film from the **side** and from **behind**, with the phone fixed so you can repeat the view.
3. Let them run **6-10 minutes** to settle, then film at least 30 seconds from each view. If symptoms come on late, film again once fatigued.
4. Review frame by frame and enter:
   - **Overstride (side):** at initial contact, drop a vertical line from the ankle bone. In front of the pelvis = Yes.
   - **Peak knee flexion in stance (side):** about 45° typical; under 40° has been linked to some knee-pain groups.
   - **Peak hip extension, vertical oscillation, foot strike (side).**
   - **Pelvic drop, knee valgus (closed knee window), crossover, heel whip (behind).**
   - **Cadence:** count right-foot contacts in 60 seconds, double it. No target is applied (about 180 is debated).

**Stop** if symptoms rise.

## 3. Countermovement jump (phone video) [P]
**Why:** tracks power change and sets the baseline for the pre-session fatigue check.
**Equipment:** phone at 120-240 fps, tripod, flat firm floor.
1. Film from the side at knee height; hands on hips throughout.
2. Warm up with a few submaximal jumps.
3. Quick dip and jump as high as possible; land on the same spot with straight legs. Do 3 jumps, 30-60 s apart.
4. On the video count frames from the last frame with feet off the ground to the first landing frame. Flight time = frames ÷ frame rate.
5. Height (cm) = 122.6 × flight time (s)². Average the 3 jumps.

**Enter:** CMJ height (cm). Skip with foot, ankle, Achilles, calf or tibia pain. Use the same phone, frame rate and counting method each time.

## 4. Standing broad jump [P]
**Equipment:** tape measure, non-slip floor, tape for the take-off line.
1. Toes behind the line, feet about hip width.
2. Swing the arms back, dip, jump forward as far as possible, land on two feet and hold.
3. Measure from the line to the nearest heel. Do 3 jumps, 1-2 minutes apart; record the best.

**Enter:** best distance (m). Skip with lower-leg pain.

## 5. Pogo hops, reactive strength (RSI) [P]
**Why:** how stiff and springy the ankle and Achilles are. RSI = flight time ÷ contact time.
**Equipment:** phone at 240 fps if possible (120 minimum), tripod, firm floor.
1. Film from the side at ankle height with the whole foot and floor in view.
2. Cue: stiff ankles, minimal knee bend, quiet landings, bounce as fast as possible.
3. Warm up with a few easy hops, then do 10 continuous hops (ignore the first 2).
4. For the 3 best-looking hops count frames for contact (lands to leaves) and flight (leaves to next landing); convert to milliseconds; average each.

**Enter:** average flight time and contact time (ms). The app calculates RSI. Do not test with lower-leg pain.

## 6. Balance reach: star excursion / Y-balance [P]
**Equipment:** floor tape, tape measure.
1. Tape three lines from a centre point: forward (anterior), back-inside (posteromedial) and back-outside (posterolateral).
2. Stand on one leg in the centre; reach the other foot as far as possible along a line, touch lightly, return without losing balance.
3. 3 practice reps per direction, then 3 recorded; keep the best (cm). Repeat on the other leg.
4. For each direction work out the left-right difference.

**Enter:** the **largest** left-right difference of the three (cm). The 4 cm flag is not from a source.

## 7. Single-leg calf raise to fatigue [P]
**Equipment:** step or flat floor, wall or rail for fingertip balance, metronome or phone timer.
1. Straight knee, fingertips only on the support, full range from lowest heel to highest rise.
2. Tempo 1 second up, 1 second down.
3. Test one leg until the range drops noticeably, the tempo is lost or the knee bends; count full-range reps.
4. Rest 2-3 minutes, test the other leg.

**Enter:** left and right reps. A difference over 10% is flagged and the weaker side gets an extra set. **Stop** on Achilles or calf pain.

## 8. Nordic hamstring lowering, break angle [P]
**Equipment:** padded surface, strap or partner to anchor the ankles, phone at 120+ fps.
1. Kneel with ankles anchored, body straight from knees to head, hands ready in front.
2. Lower as slowly as possible, hips extended.
3. Film from the side. The break point is where control of the descent is lost and the athlete falls forward; measure the body angle from the floor there. Use the best of 3.

**Enter:** break angle (degrees). Do not test with hamstring pain. Expect soreness: schedule away from key runs.

## 9. Race or time trial (separate day) [S, Daniels]
**Equipment:** track, road or treadmill, stopwatch.
1. Use a race or maximal effort from the last 4 months. A treadmill trial is fine if the same treadmill and settings are used every time.
2. Warm up, run the distance at the best sustainable effort, record distance and finishing time.

**Enter:** distance, time and days since the effort. Not for athletes under medical review for screening flags.

## 10. Optional: IMTP and CMJ force plate [P]
Not available at TPC. Leave blank. Without it the engine uses squat strength ratio and target speed.

---

## At retest
Repeat exactly the tests that were done at baseline, in the same order, at the same time of day, with the same equipment and settings. Use **New reassessment** in the athlete's case so the baseline stays unchanged.
